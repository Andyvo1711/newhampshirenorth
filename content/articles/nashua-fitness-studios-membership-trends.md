A wave of small, class-based fitness studios has opened in Nashua over the past several years, drawing members away from traditional big-box gym memberships in favor of more specialized offerings, from strength training collectives to recovery-focused studios built around stretching and mobility work.

Studio owners describe a business model built on smaller, more personal class sizes that command higher per-visit pricing than a typical gym membership, but say the tradeoff comes with real operating costs, including instructor pay that scales directly with the more hands-on coaching model these studios promote as their core differentiator.

Several owners say retention, more than acquisition, has become the primary business challenge, since the same personal relationships that make small studios appealing also mean member churn is highly sensitive to instructor turnover. Studios that have successfully retained a stable coaching staff report meaningfully better membership retention than those that have not.
