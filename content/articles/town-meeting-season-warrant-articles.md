Select boards across rural New Hampshire are finalizing warrant articles ahead of this year's town meeting season, with proposals ranging from new fire apparatus purchases to municipal broadband bonds competing for limited attention from voters accustomed to lean annual budgets.

Town administrators say the mix of articles reflects deferred maintenance catching up with small municipalities simultaneously. Several towns are proposing multi-year capital reserve fund contributions for equipment that has been patched together well past its expected service life, while others are asking voters to approve bonds for infrastructure projects that state or federal grants will only partially cover.

## Deliberative Sessions Draw Attention

Deliberative session turnout has been higher than usual in several towns this year, town moderators report, driven in part by contentious debates over proposed zoning amendments tied to housing development. Residents on both sides of these debates have organized informational meetings ahead of the formal vote, a level of organized advocacy unusual for warrant articles that in past years drew little public comment.

Town officials say the final outcomes will likely hinge on turnout at the actual meeting or ballot vote, since deliberative session debate in New Hampshire's traditional town meeting towns does not itself decide the outcome.
