A small but persistent cluster of technology startups has taken root in Manchester's former mill buildings, founders and investors say, drawn by office costs a fraction of Boston's and a tight-knit community where relationships with early customers form quickly. What the ecosystem still lacks, most agree, is the depth of venture capital available an hour south.

Several founders describe a deliberate strategy of bootstrapping further into a company's growth before seeking institutional funding, using the region's lower burn rate as a structural advantage rather than a limitation. Local angel investor groups have grown in recent years, providing early-stage capital that founders say has been essential for companies not yet ready to pitch larger venture firms.

## A Different Kind of Growth

Rather than trying to replicate Boston's startup density, economic development officials say Manchester's strategy has shifted toward attracting founders who specifically want lower costs and a slower, more sustainable growth trajectory. A handful of successful local exits in recent years have begun recycling capital and experienced operators back into the local ecosystem, a pattern founders describe as the clearest sign the cluster is maturing rather than merely persisting.
