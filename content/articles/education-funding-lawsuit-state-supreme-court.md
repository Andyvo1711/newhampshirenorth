A coalition of school districts is preparing to return to court over New Hampshire's education funding formula, arguing that the state's constitutionally required adequacy standard has failed to keep pace with the actual cost of providing a basic education, particularly for special education services and building maintenance.

The case follows a familiar arc in New Hampshire, where courts have intervened in education funding disputes multiple times since the 1990s, each time prompting legislative responses that districts have subsequently argued fall short of what the courts required. Attorneys for the district coalition say this filing includes updated cost data intended to make a more precise case than earlier challenges.

State officials have not yet filed a formal response but have signaled they will argue that recent increases in per-pupil aid, along with targeted grants for high-need districts, already address much of the underlying concern. Education policy analysts say a ruling, if the case proceeds to a decision, is unlikely before next year at the earliest, given the pace of prior education funding litigation in the state.
