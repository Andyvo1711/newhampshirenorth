New Hampshire's small commercial fishing fleet, concentrated in Portsmouth and Rye harbors, has continued to shrink in recent years, a trend fishermen attribute to a combination of rising fuel costs, shifting fish stock distributions linked to warming ocean temperatures, and federal quota systems that have grown increasingly difficult for small operators to navigate profitably.

Longtime fishermen describe species that were once reliably abundant in nearby waters moving northward as ocean temperatures rise, forcing longer trips to reach productive fishing grounds at a time when fuel represents one of the largest single costs in operating a commercial vessel. Several describe the current environment as the most challenging they have experienced in decades on the water.

Maritime heritage advocates in Portsmouth have begun documenting the fleet's history and remaining operations, motivated partly by concern that the working waterfront could disappear entirely within a generation without deliberate support. A handful of fishermen have diversified into recreational charter trips to supplement declining commercial income, a shift that reflects the broader economic pressure reshaping the Seacoast's maritime identity.
