Portsmouth's century-old Music Hall has announced a new season that programmers describe as its most ambitious in years, expanding beyond established touring headliners to include a dedicated series for regional and emerging musicians who have historically had fewer opportunities to perform on the venue's main stage.

Venue leadership says the shift reflects a deliberate effort to serve the broader Seacoast creative community rather than functioning solely as a stop on national touring circuits. The new regional series will feature a lower ticket price tier intended to build audiences for artists earlier in their careers, a model borrowed from similar historic venues in other New England cities.

## A Landmark Built for the Long Term

The Music Hall's restoration over the past several decades has been held up as a model of adaptive reuse for historic performance venues, preserving the building's original acoustics and ornate interior while updating technical infrastructure to modern touring standards. Programmers say the venue's dual identity, as both a historic landmark and a working performance space, remains central to how they think about season planning each year.
