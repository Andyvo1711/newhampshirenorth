Coastal engineers monitoring New Hampshire's eighteen-mile shoreline say recent winter storms have accelerated beach erosion in several vulnerable stretches, threatening roads, seawalls, and utility infrastructure that towns had expected to remain stable for years longer than current projections now suggest.

Municipal officials in several coastal towns say they are weighing costly seawall reinforcement projects against longer-term managed retreat strategies that would relocate the most exposed infrastructure further inland, a politically difficult conversation in communities where waterfront property values remain central to the local tax base.

State coastal resilience planners note that New Hampshire's short coastline means erosion problems, though geographically compact compared to neighboring states, are concentrated enough that a handful of severe storm seasons can meaningfully reshape the shoreline. Towns are increasingly required to factor sea level rise projections into capital planning for any infrastructure near the water, a requirement officials say has already delayed some previously planned road projects while engineers reassess elevation requirements.
