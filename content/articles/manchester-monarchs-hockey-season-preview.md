Youth hockey programs across the Manchester area say ice time has become the binding constraint on how many players they can serve, with enrollment continuing to climb even as the number of available rink hours in the region has remained essentially flat for years.

Program directors describe a waitlist dynamic that has become normal rather than exceptional, particularly for the younger age divisions where interest has grown fastest. Some families now travel to rinks in neighboring towns simply to secure practice slots that are unavailable closer to home, adding meaningfully to the time and cost commitment already required to play organized hockey.

## A Statewide Rink Shortage

Rink operators say building new ice facilities remains capital-intensive and difficult to finance without guaranteed municipal or institutional backing, meaning the shortage is unlikely to resolve quickly even as demand shows no sign of slowing. Several youth hockey associations have begun lobbying local governments to consider public-private partnerships modeled on facilities built elsewhere in New England, arguing that rink access has become a genuine barrier to participation rather than simply an inconvenience for the sport's most dedicated families.
