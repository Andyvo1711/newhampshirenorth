Lawmakers return to the State House this week facing one of the more crowded legislative calendars in recent memory, with committee chairs already signaling that housing policy, education funding, and energy costs will consume much of the session's early floor time. Leadership in both chambers say they expect several high-profile bills to draw testimony well beyond the scheduled hearing windows.

Housing bills alone number in the dozens this session, ranging from zoning reform measures aimed at increasing density near town centers to proposals that would preserve local control over land use decisions. The split reflects a broader tension in New Hampshire politics between the state's traditional deference to municipal authority and growing pressure to address a housing shortage that business groups increasingly describe as an economic emergency.

## Committee Assignments Signal Priorities

Leadership's committee assignments this session appear to favor lawmakers with direct municipal government experience, a signal that party leaders expect local impact to be a central argument in this year's debates. Several newly appointed committee chairs previously served as selectmen or town councilors before winning legislative seats.

Advocacy groups on multiple sides of the housing debate have already begun coordinating testimony schedules, suggesting hearing rooms will be full for weeks to come. Legislative staff say they do not expect final votes on the most contested bills before late in the session, given the volume of amendments already filed.
