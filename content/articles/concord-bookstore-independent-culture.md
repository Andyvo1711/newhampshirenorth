Concord's downtown independent bookstore is marking two decades in business this year, a milestone owners say required surviving two economic downturns and the broader retail disruption caused by online booksellers that put many independent shops out of business nationally over the same period.

The store's owners credit its survival to a deliberate strategy of becoming a genuine community gathering space rather than competing purely on price or selection, hosting regular author events, a long-running book club, and partnerships with local schools that bring in steady foot traffic beyond typical retail browsing.

Staff say the store's events calendar has become nearly as important to its business model as book sales themselves, with ticketed author events and workshop fees now representing a meaningful share of annual revenue. Owners describe the shift as a necessary evolution rather than a departure from the store's original mission, one they believe has helped it remain relevant as reading habits and retail patterns have both changed significantly since it first opened.
