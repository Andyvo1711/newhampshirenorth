Portsmouth's historic district commission says it is fielding an unusually high volume of applications to convert older downtown buildings into boutique hotels and short-term rental units, a trend commissioners attribute to the city's continued rise as a weekend destination for visitors from Boston and beyond.

Preservationists generally support adaptive reuse of historic buildings over demolition, but say the volume of conversion requests has raised new questions about how much of downtown's building stock should shift toward transient lodging versus long-term housing or ground-floor retail that serves year-round residents. Several recent commission hearings have drawn residents concerned about the cumulative effect on neighborhood character.

## A City Defined by Its Contrasts

City planners describe Portsmouth's ongoing challenge as balancing the economic benefit of tourism, which supports a disproportionate share of the city's restaurant and retail employment, against the risk of the downtown becoming primarily oriented toward visitors rather than the residents who have long defined its character. A working group convened by the city council is expected to recommend updated guidelines for short-term rental conversions later this year.
