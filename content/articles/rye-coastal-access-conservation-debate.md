A proposal to formalize public parking near a popular stretch of protected shoreline in Rye has divided residents ahead of an upcoming town vote, pitting advocates for expanded public beach access against neighbors and conservationists concerned about the environmental impact of increased vehicle traffic near sensitive coastal habitat.

Supporters of the plan argue that Rye's coastline, much of it in private hands or already protected as conservation land, has effectively limited public access to a small handful of locations, creating overcrowding at the few spots that remain open while nearby areas sit underused due to unclear parking rules that residents say are inconsistently enforced.

Conservation groups counter that the specific area under discussion supports nesting shorebirds during parts of the season and argue that formalizing parking would inevitably increase foot traffic through dune grass that has been slowly recovering after decades of informal use. Town officials say they hope to reach a compromise that designates specific access points while leaving the most sensitive habitat undisturbed.
