A cluster of new restaurants has opened along Dover's Central Avenue over the past two years, part of a broader pattern local business leaders describe as Dover absorbing some of the growth that has priced smaller operators out of nearby Portsmouth's increasingly expensive downtown commercial market.

Restaurant owners who relocated from Portsmouth or chose Dover over the larger city say the decision often comes down to commercial rent, which remains meaningfully lower even as Dover's own downtown has grown more competitive. Several describe Dover's customer base as more consistently local and less tourist-dependent, a tradeoff that provides steadier year-round revenue at the cost of the seasonal tourist surges Portsmouth restaurants enjoy.

City officials say the restaurant growth has coincided with new residential development downtown, creating a customer base within walking distance that has helped several newer restaurants build a loyal following faster than they might have in a district relying primarily on destination diners.
