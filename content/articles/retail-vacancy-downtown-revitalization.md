Rochester officials have expanded a downtown facade improvement grant program and streamlined permitting for small business tenants in an effort to fill a cluster of long-vacant storefronts along the city's historic Main Street corridor, part of a broader revitalization push that has been years in the making.

City planners say the vacancies date back to a wave of retail consolidation that hit the downtown hard, but note that the underlying building stock, much of it dating to the late nineteenth century, remains structurally sound and increasingly attractive to small business owners priced out of more expensive markets in Portsmouth and southern New Hampshire.

Early tenants who have signed leases under the new incentive program describe a downtown in transition, with enough existing foot traffic to support a small retail and restaurant cluster but not yet the critical mass that would make the district self-sustaining without continued city support. Officials say they view the current grant program as a multi-year commitment rather than a one-time fix.
