Estheticians working in Portsmouth say clients are asking noticeably more detailed questions about product ingredients than at any point in their careers, a shift several attribute to growing consumer awareness driven by social media discussion of skincare formulations rather than any single dramatic industry event.

Independent skincare studios say the demand has pushed many to formalize ingredient disclosure practices that were previously handled informally, publishing full ingredient lists for in-house treatments and stocking retail products with clear labeling rather than relying on brand reputation alone to reassure customers.

Estheticians caution that "clean beauty" marketing claims are not standardized by any regulatory body, meaning the term can mean different things across different product lines, and encourage customers with specific sensitivities or concerns to ask providers directly about individual ingredients rather than relying on general marketing language on packaging.
