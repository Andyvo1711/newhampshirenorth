Town officials in several rural New Hampshire communities newly connected to reliable broadband report a modest but noticeable influx of remote workers over the past two years, a shift they say has arrived with less fanfare but more staying power than the pandemic-era relocation boom that first drew attention to the trend.

Unlike the initial wave of remote movers, many of whom returned to cities once offices reopened, town officials describe the current arrivals as more deliberate, often people who researched broadband availability specifically before choosing a town, and who have since become active participants in local civic life, from serving on planning boards to volunteering with fire departments.

The shift has not been without friction. Longtime residents in a few towns have raised concerns about rising home prices attributed partly to remote-worker demand, even as town officials welcome the tax base growth and, in some cases, new civic energy that fewer year-round residents can provide in towns that had been slowly shrinking for years.
