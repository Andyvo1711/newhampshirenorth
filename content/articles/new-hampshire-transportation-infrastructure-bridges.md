State transportation officials say the backlog of municipal bridges needing significant repair or replacement continues to grow faster than current funding allows. Several bridges in central New Hampshire towns have been posted with weight restrictions in recent years, forcing school buses and emergency vehicles onto longer detour routes.

Town road agents describe a familiar dilemma: bridge repairs are far too expensive for most small municipal budgets to absorb without state or federal assistance, yet the competitive state aid programs that exist can take years to reach any individual project. In the meantime, deteriorating structures generally continue to be safe for use under posted restrictions, but the restrictions themselves create real costs, including longer emergency response times and detours that add mileage to school transportation contracts.

Legislators from both parties have expressed support for increasing dedicated bridge aid in the next capital budget, though disagreement remains over how to fund the increase without new revenue sources. Town officials say they are simply hoping any resolution arrives before more structures require full closure rather than posted limits.
