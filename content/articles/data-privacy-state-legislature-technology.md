State lawmakers are considering a consumer data privacy bill modeled in part on laws already enacted in several other states, which would give New Hampshire residents new rights to access, correct, and request deletion of personal data held by companies operating in the state.

Supporters of the bill argue New Hampshire risks becoming a regulatory outlier as more states adopt comprehensive privacy frameworks, potentially leaving residents with fewer protections than neighbors in Massachusetts or Vermont. Business groups have generally not opposed the concept but have pushed for compliance timelines and enforcement mechanisms consistent with other state laws, arguing a patchwork of incompatible state rules creates unnecessary cost for companies operating across New England.

Privacy advocates say the bill's current draft still lacks a private right of action, meaning enforcement would rest primarily with the state attorney general's office rather than allowing individual consumers to sue over violations, a gap they intend to press lawmakers to close before any final vote.
