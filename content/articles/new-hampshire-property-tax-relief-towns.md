Home values across several fast-growing towns in southern New Hampshire have climbed sharply over the past few years, and with them, property tax bills that longtime residents say are becoming difficult to sustain on fixed or modest incomes. Select boards in a handful of communities are now studying local relief programs, including expanded elderly exemptions and voluntary tax deferral agreements.

Town finance officials caution that any relief program shifts the tax burden rather than eliminating it, meaning careful modeling is required before adoption. Several towns have formed ad hoc committees to review options used elsewhere in New England, including income-based circuit breaker credits that reduce tax bills for qualifying households regardless of age.

Residents at recent public forums described the tension clearly: many support continued growth and the services it funds, but say the pace of assessment increases has outstripped their ability to plan household budgets. Town officials expect several relief proposals to appear as formal warrant articles at upcoming town meetings.
