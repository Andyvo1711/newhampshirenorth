Hospital administrators in Lebanon and the surrounding Upper Valley say staffing levels have improved modestly over the past year, aided by new nursing residency partnerships with regional colleges, but retention remains the harder and more persistent challenge. Nurses who complete training programs locally are still frequently recruited away by hospitals in Boston and Burlington offering higher pay and more predictable scheduling.

Facility leaders point to housing costs as a recurring theme in exit interviews, echoing concerns raised across other sectors of the state's economy. A newly hired nurse earning a competitive regional wage can still struggle to find affordable housing within a reasonable commute of the hospital campus, particularly in a market where rental vacancy rates remain historically low.

Several health systems have begun experimenting with housing stipends and partnerships with local developers to set aside units for healthcare workers, an approach borrowed from programs already used in some Vermont hospital systems. Whether these efforts meaningfully change retention will likely not be clear for another full budget cycle, but administrators describe the current approach as a necessary experiment given the stakes for patient care capacity.
